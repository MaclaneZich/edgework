## Critical

### m6-p22 (solution, displayed algebra wrong)
- **What is wrong.** The particular-solution step read "$\alpha k = 1 + \alpha(pk + p - qk + q)$". Expanding $p(k+1) + q(k-1)$ gives $pk + p + qk - q$; the q terms had the wrong signs. The conclusion ($0 = 1 + \alpha(p-q)$, $\alpha = -5$) was right, but the written step does not produce it.
- **Fix.** Rewritten as $\alpha k = 1 + \alpha[p(k+1) + q(k-1)] = 1 + \alpha k + \alpha(p - q)$, which reduces to $0 = 1 + \alpha(p-q)$.

## Major

### m6-p28 (prompt ambiguous about the shutdown tick)
- **What is wrong.** The prompt said "No cost is paid for the tick on which it is shut down." A strong student reads the shutdown tick as the tick that starts at inventory $\pm 2$ and moves to $\pm 3$, so that tick would cost nothing. The solution (and the verifier) charge $|k|$ at the start of every tick including that one, and only skip a charge at $\pm 3$. Under the other reading the cost system is $c_2 = 0 + \tfrac12 c_1$, giving a different answer.
- **Fix.** Prompt now says the cost is charged at the start of every tick on the inventory at that moment, gives the explicit example that a tick starting at 2 costs \$2 whatever happens next, and states that no further cost is charged after reaching $\pm 3$ because no tick starts there. `commonMistake` reworded to match ("charging a cost of 3 at the moment of shutdown").

### Lesson card "Sanity checks that catch wrong equations" (misleading example)
- **What is wrong.** The example of a near miss that still contains a prefix read "(HTH after HT then H holds an H)". HT followed by H *completes* HTH; it is not a near miss at all, so the example teaches the wrong thing.
- **Fix.** Replaced with two correct examples: waiting for UDDU, the history UD followed by U still holds a U (state 1, not 0); waiting for HH, a T after an H goes to 0, not back to "H".

### Plain `"..."` strings containing KaTeX backslashes (7 hints; one rendering bug)
- **What is wrong.** Hint 2 of m6-p06 (line 198) was a plain string containing `$\frac{1}{6}$`; JavaScript turns `\f` into a form feed, so the hint rendered as a form feed followed by `rac{1}{6}`. Six other hints (p10 h1, p11 h2, p14 h3, p15 h1, p17 h1, lc4 h1) used double-escaped `\\frac` / `\\dots` / `\\,`, which render correctly but violate the String.raw rule and now fail the verifier's scan.
- **Fix.** All seven converted to String.raw with single backslashes.
