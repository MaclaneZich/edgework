## Issues

### m3-p23 — minor (wording / displayed formula)
**What:** `answer.exact` read `\frac{1 - (11/12)^2}{4(1 - (23/24)^2)} = \frac{23/576}{47/576}`. The first expression equals 23/47, but its numerator is 23/144, not 23/576, so the displayed intermediate did not follow from the displayed formula.
**Fix:** rewrote as `\frac{\frac{1}{4}(1 - (11/12)^2)}{1 - (23/24)^2} = \frac{23/576}{47/576} = \frac{23}{47}`, which is P(both boys and a March boy) / P(at least one March boy) with matching intermediates. Value unchanged.

### m3-p24 — minor (close paraphrase of a known problem)
**What:** "Every car is blue or green, 20% blue, hit-and-run, witness correct with probability 0.8" is the classic blue/green cab problem's framing and reliability figure almost verbatim; only the three-witness twist was new. CONTENT_GUIDE requires a new framing.
**Fix:** reframed as a courier fleet of silver/orange vans (20% orange) that knocked over a bicycle rack in the depot yard, with three staff witnesses. All numbers identical, so the verified answer 1/2 and the verifier are untouched; prompt, hints, solution (reports now written OOS), steps and variation updated consistently.

### m3-p08 — minor (commonMistake wording)
**What:** "Treating BG and GB as a single outcome ... which makes the three survivors unequal" was muddled: collapsing BG/GB leaves two survivors, not three, and the error is treating those two as equally likely.
**Fix:** reworded to say exactly that ("one of each" is twice as likely as GG).

### m3-p02 — minor (prompt precision)
**What:** "You learn that at least one of the dice shows an even number" did not say that this is all you learn; a careful student could ask whether a specific die was indicated (which would give 1/2, the variation's answer).
**Fix:** prompt now reads "You learn only that at least one of the dice shows an even number (nothing about which die)", matching the style of p05.

### Lesson card "Base rates and false positives" — minor (over-general rule of thumb)
**What:** "when prevalence is below the false-positive rate, most positives are false" is false for large prevalence (e.g. prevalence 40%, false-positive rate 50%, sensitivity 1 gives 0.40 true vs 0.30 false positives). It is correct for rare conditions, which is the card's context.
**Fix:** qualified it as a rule of thumb for rare conditions and added the one-line reason (true positives are at most the prevalence; false positives are nearly the full false-positive rate).
