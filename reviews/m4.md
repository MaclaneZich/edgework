## Issues

### m4-p01 (minor, fixed)
**What:** The variation said "What is the expected total prize value, given that two tickets can never win the same prize?" A strong student reads "given that" as a conditioning clause.
**Fix:** Reworded to a plain question followed by a parenthetical note that the two prizes are dependent. Variation answer (1.45) unchanged and recomputed correct.

### m4-p12 (minor, fixed)
**What:** Solution closed with "the first stamp is free", which can be read as stage 1 costing zero visits; it costs exactly one.
**Fix:** "the first visit always yields a new design (stage 1 costs exactly one visit)".

### m4-p15 (minor, fixed)
**What:** The prompt never said the 12 flips are independent, and the solution's P(change) = 2pq relies on it. CONTENT_GUIDE asks for "independent" to be stated when it matters.
**Fix:** Added "the flips being independent" to the prompt.

### m4-lc1 (minor, fixed)
**What:** Same omission ("You flip it 10 times"). Here independence is not needed for the mean, and the solution says so, but stating it keeps the sample space unambiguous.
**Fix:** "You flip it 10 times independently."

### Lesson, card "Expected maximum and minimum of dice" (minor, fixed)
**What:** Sentence 2 says E[min] = 7 - E[max] for any number of dice; sentence 3 opened "For two dice only, max + min = X + Y, so E[max] + E[min] = 7 exactly", which a reader can take as contradicting the previous line (as if the sum were 7 only for two dice). The "only" was meant to qualify the pointwise identity, not the conclusion.
**Fix:** "With two dice there is a second reason for E[max] + E[min] = 7: the pointwise identity max + min = X + Y, which has no analogue for three or more dice."

### Lesson, card "When linearity is not enough" (minor, fixed)
**What:** (a) "Paying 60/X on a die roll is worth 24.5" is ambiguous about who pays. (b) "E[max(X, Y)] >= max(E[X], E[Y]), with equality only in degenerate cases" is imprecise: equality holds whenever one variable is almost surely at least the other (e.g. Y = X + 1), which is not degenerate.
**Fix:** (a) "A game that pays 60/X on a die roll is worth 24.5". (b) "with equality only when one variable is almost surely at least the other".
