## Major

### m10-p04 (solution, option discussion) - false statement contradicting the variation
- What was wrong: "Option 3 (1/2) would be right only if the host had opened two boxes." If the host opens two empty boxes the single remaining box carries 3/4 (which is exactly what the problem's own variationAnswer says), so 1/2 is right in no version of this setup.
- Fix: reworded to explain why 1/2 is impossible (your box keeps 1/4, so the other two closed boxes share only 3/4).

### Lesson card "The clock" + "The day before" + lc1 - internally inconsistent pacing
- What was wrong: the card is titled "six minutes a question" and asks "am I ahead or behind the six-minute pace?", but the same card budgets 15 minutes for coding. 11 x 6 + 15 = 81 > 75: a student who follows the stated pace runs out of time. The body itself computes 5.5 minutes per math question, and the "day before" card recommends a 60-minute timer for 11 problems (5.45 each), so the content disagreed with itself.
- Fix: card retitled to five and a half minutes; body states the 81-minute arithmetic explicitly and calls six the ceiling, not the pace; the clock-check question and the "day before" pacing bullet now say five and a half minutes for math and fifteen for coding; lc1's solution now says "a full minute under the planned pace" instead of "under the six-minute pace". The worked lc1 scenario (7 x 6 + 15) is unchanged and still correct.

### Lesson cards "Sanity checks" and "When to guess" - false general rule about posteriors
- What was wrong: "Evidence for a hypothesis moves its probability up from the prior, never past the likelihood" and "A posterior after strong evidence is between the prior and the likelihood." Not true in general: with prior 0.9, P(E|H) = 0.5, P(E|H^c) = 0.1 the posterior is 0.978 > 0.5. It happens to hold for p03 (rare prior, non-trivial false-positive rate), which is presumably where it came from, but as a sanity check it would reject correct answers.
- Fix: both bullets now say the posterior moves from the prior in the direction of the evidence, with the size set by the likelihood ratio, and give the p03 numbers (2% prior, 95% sensitivity, posterior near 0.24) as the illustration; the "guess" bullet scopes the "far below sensitivity" claim to rare hypotheses with non-trivial false-positive rates.
