## Issues

### M2-01  (major)  18 hints stored as plain `"..."` strings with KaTeX backslashes  — FIXED
- Where: `src/content/problems/m2.ts`, hints of p06 (hints 1, 3), p07 (3), p13 (3), p15 (2, 3),
  p16 (3), p17 (1, 3), p20 (1), p23 (2), p24 (1, 2), p27 (2, 3), p28 (3), lc1 (2, 3).
- What is wrong: the strings were ordinary double-quoted literals, so JavaScript consumed the
  backslashes before KaTeX ever saw them. Confirmed with node: `\frac` becomes form-feed +
  `rac`, `\binom` becomes backspace + `inom`, `\ne` becomes newline + `e`, `\times` becomes
  tab + `imes`, and `\cap`, `\le`, `\mid`, `\max`, `\cdot` lose the backslash entirely
  (`$P(A cap B) = <FF>rac{3}{36}$`). Every one of those hints would have rendered as broken
  math. `tsc` does not flag this, and the verifier only checks numbers, so nothing caught it.
- Fix: converted all 18 hints to `String.raw` template literals (no text changes other than
  M2-02 and M2-04 below). Grep for `"[^"]*\\` in the file now returns nothing.
- Note for the parent: `src/content/problems/m6.ts` line 198 has the same defect
  (`$\frac{1}{6}$` in a plain string); it is outside this review's scope and was not edited.

### M2-02  (major)  p14 hint 2 misclassifies the recursion cases  — FIXED
- Where: m2-p14 "Three heads in a row somewhere", hint 2.
- What is wrong: the hint said to classify sequences "by how the sequence ends: $T$, $HT$ or
  $HHT$ (or the whole thing is at most two heads)". Those three endings are nested (every
  string ending in $HHT$ also ends in $T$), so they do not partition anything, and the
  parenthetical is irrelevant for $n \ge 3$. The solution's actual recursion classifies by
  the trailing block of heads (ends in $T$, $TH$, $THH$), which is what gives
  $a_n = a_{n-1} + a_{n-2} + a_{n-3}$. A student following the hint literally would be led
  away from the solution.
- Fix: hint 2 now reads "Classify by the trailing block of heads: the sequence ends in $T$,
  $TH$ or $THH$, and everything before that $T$ is a shorter sequence with no $HHH$."

### M2-03  (minor)  p27 hint 3 compared an outcome with a number  — FIXED
- "it is also the single outcome (3, 4), which is not $\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{6}\cdot\frac{1}{6}$"
  compared an outcome to a probability. Reworded to "so its probability $\frac{1}{36}$ is not
  $\frac{1}{6} \cdot \frac{1}{6} \cdot \frac{1}{6}$".

### M2-04  (minor)  p27 structure mirrors the canonical textbook example  — NOT CHANGED
- "Total is 7", "red die is 3", "blue die is 4" is the standard illustration of pairwise but
  not mutual independence (the common textbook version uses first die 4 / second die 3).
  The wording here is original and the module's concept card uses the same trio, so it is
  consistent; the idea is a classic and allowed by the guide. Changing the faces (e.g. red 2,
  blue 5) would require editing the verifier, which this review was told not to do for a
  non-answer issue. Flagged only.

### M2-05  (minor)  Lesson worked examples duplicate p17 and p23 almost verbatim  — NOT CHANGED
- Worked example 1 is m2-p17 (one pair from the nine-card deck) and worked example 2 is
  m2-p23 (gold coin turn-taking), with the same numbers and steps. Not an error, and the
  guide does not forbid it, but a student who reads the lesson first gets those two practice
  problems for free. Consider swapping one of them for a fresh instance (e.g. 3 gold among 9
  coins, which gives 11/21 and is still a clean sum) if the bank's integrity matters.

### M2-06  (minor)  p25 fastPath states a monotonicity without justification  — NOT CHANGED
- "$1 - (1 - 1/(2n))^n$ falls with $n$" is true (the sequence $(1 - x/n)^n$ increases to
  $e^{-x}$) but unargued. Acceptable for a fast path; noted only.
